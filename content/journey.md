## Chapter 1 - school days

I grew up in small town called [Padra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padra) in Vadodara district of Gujarat. Studied in Gujarati medium in privet school.

During my school days I was interested in learning languages, writing poems, doing theater. I wanted to become journalist so I can write on different topics, I have also created handwritten magazine in Gujarati called 'Samvad'. 

I have not been to a formal school after 10th grade. I have been working with children since the age of 18.

After 10th grade I wanted to study subject like Gujarati literature, agriculture, psychology, creative writing / advertising but... 

I took admission in science stream because family pressure. I bunked school and I spent time exploring bloging, website and software development (linux, free and open source software and technology world), sales and marketing, theater, writing, entrepreneurship and many more interests.

failed in all subjects, took admission in diploma mechanical engineering. Again bunked classes and started volunteering at different places, working with children.

Later, I completed 12th grade and a BA in Sociology as an external student.

## Chapter 2 - Self designed learning journey

I started working with children by sharing how to use a computer, inspired by the [Hole in the Wall project by Sugata Mitra ↗](https://www.edutopia.org/blog/self-organized-learning-sugata-mitra). I explored [MIT Scratch ↗](https://scratch.mit.edu/), made [Arvind Gupta toys ↗](https://www.arvindguptatoys.com/), told stories, played games and more.

Slowly, after lots of experimentation, I realized two things:

*   I like being with children and young adults. I can be myself.
*   I can do creative experiments and explorations with them, which I can't do in school or university.

I started volunteering in different schools, meeting inspiring, passionate educators and children, visiting different learning spaces across India, reading books and attending workshops. That is how my journey as an educator started, and it is evolving every day.

During this decade long journey, I have worked in formal and informal learning spaces across India, and made friends with many children and educators.

I was primarily interested in creative practice, but I was not courageous and privilege enough to find space in the mainstream arts / design community. (I didn't even know about design education) Instead, I found my space with children and young adults. I like being with them and exploring different creative processes with them. Slowly, through reading, reflecting and embodied experiences, my philosophy of education emerged, where I try to find pedagogies exploring the aim of education as ‘education for harmony’.

## Chapter 3 - current work

My work is non linear and lies at the intersections of educational [philosophy](/philosophy), [pedagogy/learning experience design](/educator) and [creative practices](/artist).

With 13 years of practice across formal and informal learning spaces in India, I facilitate transformative learning experiences with children and young adults through experiential pedagogies.

I’m a recipient of the Earth Educator fellowship, Rewilding Storyteller fellowship and a faculty member for the ‘Child Development, Learning & Special Needs’ course by Eklavya Foundation.

As an artist I am interested in writing, theater and multi sensorial storytelling. Most of the time my creative practice get intertwined with my educational practice. I’ve self-published two books in Gujarati, and I’m currently co-publishing a children’s magazine called Patrango.

**My work is rooted in a deeper question:** what does education look like when our collective purpose is to live together harmoniously?
